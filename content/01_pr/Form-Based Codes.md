Since 2017, I have worked in interdisciplinary urban planning and architecture consultancy studio, where I began as an urban planner and strategist and later became a project lead. My work has mostly focused on urban policy and form-based design codes. I have developed planning and urban design regulations covering land use, development density, building height and massing, setbacks, street frontage and public realm interfaces, as well as architectural articulation, materials, colour palettes, landscaping, and other design parameters.

Urban policy is still often embedded in static PDF documents and specialist workflows. My projects explored how planning regulations could operate differently when translated into computational and interactive formats, experimenting with generative databases, custom Rhino and QGIS plugins, and web-based tools for residents and municipal staff.

In 2020 and 2021, I have worked in Derbent on the implementation of the city’s Masterplan, focusing on the tension between urban growth and heritage protection. Much of this work involved translating broad planning and conservation principles into regulations that could actually be applied to individual sites and buildings. 

The approach was based on typology-based zoning, where the city was divided according to existing and proposed urban types, such as terraced housing or mixed-use development. Each typology defined its own parameters for land use, building height, density and urban form in relation to the surrounding context. We translated these parameters into Digital Design Codes that also addressed materials and construction methods, and connected them to a digital planning tool used by municipal staff and residents.

## Building Passports: Integrating Building Data into Regional GIS

Developed a digital Building Passport system to guide building design and support long-term maintenance in Moscow region. Design requirements were linked to individual building addresses, allowing regulatory and architectural data to be integrated into the regional GIS. Through a web-based interface, users could review and adjust design parameters and automatically generate a Building Passport, making the approval process more transparent and easier to navigate.

## A101: Urban Guidelines

Develop tech-enabled Digital Design Codes to guide the city's sprawl and regeneration through detailed regulations of land use, density, materials used and construction methods that preserve its unique visual and cultural identity.

|           |                                                                       |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Timeframe | 2018                                                                  |
| Location  | Saint Petersburg, Moscow                                              |
| Area      | 6,963 hectares (municipal district)                                   |
| My role   | Urban Designer                                                        |
| Team      | [unit4.io](https://unit4.io/en), [MLA+ SPb](https://www.mlaplus.com/) |
| Link      | https://unit4.io/scnd-gb                                              |
  
Urban Guidelines is a framework that defines the main principles of spatial, social and economic development. The guidelines justify and fix the meanings and values that are important for their material embodiment. I was involved in all stages of the project, from discussing the methodology and preparing spatial analytics to systematising and creating a design solutions database that allows the testing of design decisions.
